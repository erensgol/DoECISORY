Statistical Diagnostics

This section explains how to interpret the diagnostic metrics, ANOVA tables, multicollinearity screening, and cross-validation indicators generated by Lib_Vise and presented in the automated analysis report.

1. Statistical Report Structure

Through VISE_GenerateScientificReport_DDEF, a structured seven-stage audit report is compiled:

  • Stage I: Design Vitals: Orthogonality, condition number, and D/A/G/I optimality metrics.
  • Stage II: Model Summary: $R^2$, Adjusted $R^2$, Prediction $Q^2$ (Cross-Validated $R^2$), PRESS, and AIC.
  • Stage III: Analysis of Variance (ANOVA): $F$-statistics, term $p$-values, and Lack-of-Fit test.
  • Stage IV: Regression Coefficients: Parameter estimates, standard errors, $t$-tests, and VIF multicollinearity screening.
  • Stage V: Factor Sensitivity: Normalised partial derivatives and relative factor importance ranking.
  • Stage VI: Multi-Objective Optimisation: Composite desirability scores and Candidate Pool ranking.
  • Stage VII: Decay Audit: Summary of precursor and product decay corrections applied.

2. Model Fit and Validation: $R^2$ and $Q^2$

A high Coefficient of Determination ($R^2$) is necessary but insufficient to prove model validity. Standard polynomial regressions are susceptible to overfitting, where the fitted surface tracks experimental noise rather than underlying chemical relationships.

2.1. Metrics Comparison

MetricSymbolMathematical BasisDiagnostic Role
Coefficient of Determination$R^2$$1 - \frac{SS_{\text{res}}}{SS_{\text{tot}}}$Measures explained variance within the training points. Monotonically increases with added model terms.
Adjusted $R^2$$R^2_{\text{adj}}$$1 - \frac{SS_{\text{res}} / (N - p)}{SS_{\text{tot}} / (N - 1)}$Penalises unnecessary model terms based on residual degrees of freedom.
Prediction $Q^2$$Q^2$$1 - \frac{\text{PRESS}}{SS_{\text{tot}}}$Leave-One-Out (LOO) cross-validated predictive ability across unseen data points.

2.2. Decision Rules

  • Robust Model: $R^2 > 0.85$, $Q^2 > 0.70$, and $R^2 - Q^2 < 0.20$. The model has genuine predictive capability.
  • Overfitting Warning: $R^2 \approx 0.95$ while $Q^2 < 0.50$ (or negative). Indicates that higher-order interaction or quadratic terms are modelling random experimental fluctuation. In such cases, backward term elimination or linear model simplification should be considered.

3. Model Sufficiency: Lack-of-Fit Testing

In experimental designs containing replicated centre points, the total residual sum of squares ($SS_{\text{res}}$) is partitioned into two orthogonal components:

\[SS_{\text{res}} = SS_{\text{pure error}} + SS_{\text{lack-of-fit}}\]

  • Pure Error ($SS_{\text{pure error}}$): Variance observed exclusively among replicate runs performed at identical factor levels.
  • Lack-of-Fit ($SS_{\text{lack-of-fit}}$): Systematic divergence between the fitted mathematical surface and the true response curvature.

3.1. Interpretation

The Lack-of-Fit $F$-statistic compares mean squares:

\[F_{\text{LoF}} = \frac{MS_{\text{lack-of-fit}}}{MS_{\text{pure error}}}\]

  • $p_{\text{LoF}} > 0.05$ (Not Significant): The model adequately captures the response surface. Systematic divergence does not exceed pure experimental noise.
  • $p_{\text{LoF}} < 0.05$ (Significant Lack-of-Fit): Indicates model inadequacy. If fitting a linear model, progression to a quadratic response surface model or inclusion of interaction terms is required.

4. Multicollinearity & Variance Inflation Factor (VIF)

Multicollinearity arises when predictor columns in the expanded model matrix are linearly correlated, inflating coefficient variances and making parameter estimates unstable.

For each model term $j$, DoECISORY computes the Variance Inflation Factor (VIF):

\[\text{VIF}_j = \frac{1}{1 - R_j^2}\]

where $R_j^2$ is the coefficient of determination obtained by regressing term $x_j$ against all remaining $p-1$ predictor terms.

4.1. Analytical Guidance

  • $\text{VIF} \approx 1.0$: Ideal orthogonal property (characteristic of standard Box-Behnken or Fractional designs in coded space).
  • $1.0 < \text{VIF} < 5.0$: Moderate, acceptable correlation. Standard errors remain stable.
  • $\text{VIF} > 5.0$ (or $> 10.0$): Severe collinearity. Common when un-centred physical coordinates are regressed directly without coded scaling, or when severe space clipping has occurred. Recommend checking factor ranges or eliminating redundant interaction terms.

5. Candidate Selection Criteria

When evaluating the Candidate Pool generated by VISE_RunOptimisation_DDEF, the platform presents a portfolio of viable formulations rather than enforcing an isolated mathematical summit. The decision protocol guides selection based on real-world benchtop priorities:

5.1. Classification Criteria

Candidate IdentifierOrigin & Selection LogicPrimary Laboratory ObjectiveTypical Trade-off
TOP-01*Global metaheuristic peak (BlackBoxOptim) in continuous space.Uncompromised multi-objective desirability.May require strict environmental/equipment tolerances.
TOP-02Top-scoring node from high-density discrete grid evaluation.Discrete verification of surface convexity.Slight discretization offset relative to BBO peak.

INP-ReactionTimeMinimal factor 2 coordinate within $\ge 90\% D_{\max}$ tier.Minimise radioactive decay loss and synthesis time.Slightly narrower chemical conversion margin.

OUT-YieldMaximal predicted primary response within $\ge 90\% D_{\max}$ tier.Prioritise total chemical recovery.May tolerate slightly elevated secondary impurities.
OUT-ImpurityMinimal predicted impurity response within $\ge 90\% D_{\max}$ tier.Strict regulatory compliance for injectable purity.May require slightly lower operating temperature.

5.2. Selection Logic

  1. Unconstrained Baseline (TOP-01*): Inspect the global optimum. If temperatures, pressures, and reagent volumes fall comfortably inside standard operating procedures, advance with TOP-01*.
  2. Reagent or Thermal Constraints: If precursor synthesis is challenging or the substrate is heat-sensitive, evaluate INP-Precursor or INP-Temperature. If INP-Precursor provides $D = 0.91$ while reducing active peptide consumption by $30\%$, it represents an economically and operationally superior formulation.
  3. Purity or Safety Imperatives: For clinical batch release where radiochemical purity must exceed regulatory thresholds (e.g. $\ge 95\%$), select OUT-Impurity or OUT-Colloid.
  4. Transition Commitment: Feeding the selected candidate into FLOW_BuildIPKT_DDEF establishes the anchor coordinate for sequential Phase 2 design space refinement.
